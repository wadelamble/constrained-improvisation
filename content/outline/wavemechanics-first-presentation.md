## Codex

Yes. That is a coherent field-first architecture for the manuscript.

The one qualification is that a function representation is not automatically wave mechanics. But a **complex, linear function representation of continuous translations**, with phase and superposition, essentially is the mathematical structure of wave mechanics.

The conceptual chain becomes:

```math
\text{symmetry}
\rightarrow
\text{function representations}
\rightarrow
\text{waves and Fourier modes}
\rightarrow
\text{spacetime and dispersion}
\rightarrow
\text{variational and Hamiltonian mechanics}
\rightarrow
\text{QM and QFT}.
```

On that telling:

- Spacetime symmetry organizes how waves transform.
- Lagrangian mechanics selects stationary field histories.
- Hamiltonian mechanics describes movement through the space of field configurations.
- Quantum mechanics assigns amplitudes to those configurations.
- QFT treats fields and their excitations as fundamental.
- “Particles” enter later as countable field excitations associated with discrete, localized measurement events.
- Rigid bodies emerge still later as localized, decohered, collective classical limits of field states.

This avoids pretending that physics begins with tiny hard objects and then mysteriously discovers waves. It begins with the structure rich enough to describe nature—fields, functions, phase, and symmetry—and derives particle-like trajectories and rigid objects as limiting appearances.

The only wording I would guard is “particles are measurement outcomes.” Better: particle language identifies **countable field excitations that produce particle-like measurement outcomes**. In QFT, particle states remain real mathematical structures, particularly as asymptotic states, even though they are not generally the deepest ontology.
