- **Quantum evolution:** The distinction between probabilistic measurement outcomes and deterministic evolution of a specified isolated quantum state is sound. The pool-ball statement describes the regime of classical predictability; it does not assert that every macroscopic quantum state must have narrowly concentrated probabilities.
- **State-action invariance:** Footnote 5 is mathematically correct. Writing \(D=i\hbar\partial_t-\hat H\), differentiation gives
  \[
  i\hbar\dot W_g=\hat H W_g-W_g\hat H,
  \qquad D(W_g\Psi)=W_gD\Psi.
  \]
  Consequently, unitarity makes the action integrand invariant for trial histories, without using their equations of motion. The construction works for arbitrary unitary transformations of initial data, so it would not independently establish relativistic physics. The excerpt explicitly acknowledges that limitation and assumes the required Poincaré representation. I therefore do **not** regard this as a false claim of Lorentz invariance.
- **Particle labels:** The discussion correctly separates the symmetry-imposed form of the free-particle dispersion relation from the empirically determined mass. Footnotes 6 and 7 also avoid two common errors: treating a spinor’s overall sign as directly observable, and identifying electric charge with the universal overall-phase redundancy.
